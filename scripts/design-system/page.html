Eight agents, one glance

No cards, no pills. A row is a glyph, a title and its facts, and the one you open takes a plum wash.

Six rules. No boxes.

Every screen in Nekko Agent follows the same six rules. A survey in November found 353 bordered boxes in the app. These rules are how they left.

Read why

First, it fades in.

Menus and popovers arrive over {{light:dur-med}} on one curve, {{light:ease-out}}.

Then it rises a little.

Four pixels for a popover, eight at the very most. Travel is a hint, not a ride.

It never bounces.

No springs, no overshoot, no blur. A press scales to 0.97 in {{light:dur-fast}} and comes straight back.

And it leaves in 150.

Exits run about a quarter faster than entrances. Reduce Motion keeps only the fades.

Yes. Nekko Agent is MIT licensed, tokens included. Take the values, the rules and the checklist. The cat stays with us.

Boxes inside boxes are noise. Space and type do the grouping, a border is the last resort, and there is never a card in a card.

Plum is Nekko's own colour and it means intent: the one primary button, focus, what is selected. Petrol sits across the wheel for information, so the two never compete. We tried a plum dark called Night; the primary button vanished into it.

Buttons are 28 tall for a pointer and grow to 44 under a finger. The iPhone and Android apps keep their platform's symbols and share the colourways, the mascot and the words.

Every agent working on Nekko Agent reads the design system before touching a screen. This page is also served as Markdown and JSON, so yours can read it too.
