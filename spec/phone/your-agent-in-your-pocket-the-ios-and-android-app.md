# Your agent in your pocket (the iOS and Android app)

**Availability** `[limited beta, 2026-10-24]`: the native iOS app ([spec](native-ios-app.md)) and the native Android app ([spec](native-android-app.md)) are both available in a **limited beta**. Neither is on public sale: the App Store and Google Play releases are still to come (#615), and so is a public beta. The iOS beta goes out through TestFlight (`.github/workflows/ios-testflight.yml` uploads from `main`).

`[in build 2026-10-03]` A native app for iPhone and Android with three tabs: **Chats**, **On this phone**, and **Computers**. It is built for one-handed check-ins rather than as a copy of the desktop.

- **First run.** Nekko and two choices: *Pair your computer* (scan the QR from Settings → Remote access, or paste the link) or *Chat on this phone* (download a model). Notification permission is asked only after a computer is paired, never at first launch.
- **Chats** lists the computer's chats (newest first, with the last reply as a preview) and the phone's own chats in a second section. A chat that is mid-run shows **Working**; one waiting on an approval or a question shows **Needs you**, including approvals that were already waiting when the phone connected. A line at the top says whether the computer is online, offline (asleep, app closed) or reconnecting, and turns into "pair again" if the computer removed this phone. Pull to refresh.
- **Starting a chat** asks where it should run. On the computer: its default model is preselected, any of its providers' models can be picked, and the chat can be pointed at one of its project folders. On the phone: any downloaded model.
- **A computer chat** shows the conversation as the desktop does, compactly: replies render Markdown, the model's reasoning folds away, each tool step is one line (its file, command or query, with a status mark) that expands to its output. Replies stream as they are written. When the agent wants to do something risky, an **Allow this?** card shows the exact command and the risk level, with Approve and Deny, and the phone buzzes. When the agent asks a question, its options appear as tappable choices with a free-text answer and Skip. While a run is going, a new message queues as a follow-up, exactly as on the desktop; the send button becomes Stop. Reconnecting after the phone slept re-reads the chat, so nothing streamed in the meantime is lost.
- **On this phone** is a short, curated list of models that run on the phone itself through llama.cpp (Metal on iPhone, OpenCL or CPU on Android): Qwen3.5 0.8B, 2B and 4B, Gemma 4 E2B and E4B, and Llama 3.2 1B. Each shows its exact download size, license, and how well it fits this phone's memory (*Runs great*, *Runs well*, *Tight fit*, or *Too big*, which cannot be downloaded); one is marked **Recommended** for the phone's memory, the everyday 2B where it fits comfortably and the tiny one otherwise. Downloads come from Hugging Face with live progress and can be cancelled; a download that arrives short is discarded rather than loaded. Downloaded models can be made the default or deleted.
- **A phone chat** runs entirely on the device with no network and nothing leaving the phone. The model loads on first use (with progress), replies stream, generation speed shows under the last reply, and models that can reason get a **Think** switch (off by default, for speed). One model is in memory at a time. Phone chats are saved on the phone only.
- **Computers** lists paired computers with their connection state, relay and pairing age; the first connection names a computer after its OS ("Windows PC", "Mac"), and it can be renamed, switched to, reconnected, or forgotten (which deletes the key from the phone and reminds the user to remove the phone on the computer too). Several computers can be paired; the app talks to one at a time.
- **Security** is the remote-control model above, unchanged: pairing keys and this phone's identity live in the iOS Keychain / Android Keystore, the encryption key is derived once on pairing and cached there, the one-time code is never resent after the first welcome, and provider API keys the computer returns are dropped on arrival and never stored on the phone.

Where remote control is the whole app on a phone, **messaging channels** are the lighter touch: messaging the agent the way you message a person, from an app you already have open. Configured under **Connectors → Messaging channels**, a channel long-polls its provider (no public URL, no inbound ports) and each allowed chat binds to one Nekko Agent session, so a reply in that conversation continues it rather than starting over. The agent's answer streams into a single message that keeps editing itself instead of one message per delta; approvals and the agent's questions arrive as inline buttons and resolve with a tap, and a turn started in the desktop app on a bound session mirrors its reply into the channel. Trust is explicit: channels are off by default, and only the listed chat ids can reach the agent, everything else is refused before a session exists. Telegram is the first adapter; Slack and WhatsApp Business follow the same seam.
